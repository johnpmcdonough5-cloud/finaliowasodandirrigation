What the First Hard Freeze Means for Your Lawn and Irrigation System

A few fall steps keep your turf strong through the cold months and ready to green up fast in spring.

One freeze, two very different things happening on your property. Your lawn and your sprinkler system respond to the first hard freeze in completely different ways.

For the Lawn: Dormancy, Not Damage

Grass is built to survive freezing temperatures. Once the first hard freeze hits, cool-season turf grass goes dormant, meaning growth stops and the blades brown, but the plant itself is fine. This is a normal, expected part of the yearly cycle, not something to worry about or try to prevent.

For Irrigation: The Opposite Problem

A sprinkler system has no equivalent to dormancy. Water sitting in pipes, valves, and the backflow device doesn't go dormant, it freezes and expands, and that expansion is what cracks fittings. Unlike the lawn, the irrigation system needs to be actively prepared before the freeze arrives, not left to handle it on its own.

Why the Same Freeze Requires Two Different Responses

The lawn's response to the first freeze is automatic and requires nothing from you. The irrigation system's response is entirely dependent on whether the water was removed beforehand. That's the core reason blowouts are treated as a hard deadline while lawn dormancy isn't something anyone schedules around.

In eastern Iowa, that first hard freeze typically lands early to mid October, which is the point where these two different stories, one automatic and one that needs action, both begin.

What to Actually Do Before the Freeze

The lawn doesn't need intervention going into dormancy. The irrigation system does. If the blowout hasn't happened yet by mid October, that's the piece of the property still exposed to freeze damage.

Common questions

Does my lawn need protection from the first freeze?

No. Grass goes dormant as a normal response to cold and doesn't need intervention to survive winter.

Why does the sprinkler system need action but the lawn doesn't?

Water left in the system doesn't go dormant, it freezes and expands, which damages pipes and fittings. Grass has a built-in way of handling cold that irrigation systems don't.

When does the first hard freeze usually happen here?

Typically early to mid October in eastern Iowa, though the exact date shifts year to year.
