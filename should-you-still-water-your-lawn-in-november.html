Should You Still Water Your Lawn in November?

A few fall steps keep your turf strong through the cold months and ready to green up fast in spring.

Watering habits built up over summer don't just switch off overnight, but by November, most lawns need a lot less than muscle memory suggests.

Established Lawns Need Very Little by November

Once grass goes dormant for winter, it isn't actively growing, and its water needs drop accordingly. An established lawn that's already dormant by November generally doesn't need supplemental watering, since natural precipitation is typically enough at this point in the season.

New Sod Is a Different Situation

Sod installed earlier in fall may still be finishing establishment if it went in later in the season. If the ground hasn't fully frozen yet and the sod hasn't clearly gone dormant, it can still benefit from watering support, though nowhere near the frequency it needed right after installation.

How to Tell Which Situation You're In

Dormant grass is brown and not actively growing. If your lawn, new or established, has already browned off for the season, watering isn't doing much for it anymore. If parts of a fall install are still green and clearly still growing, that section may still be in an active establishment phase.

Once the Ground Freezes, the Question Answers Itself

Once the ground is frozen, watering isn't practical or useful regardless of the lawn's status. Eastern Iowa's first hard freeze typically arrives early to mid October, and by November, most years, the ground has already made this decision for you.

Common questions

Does an established lawn need watering in November?

Generally no, once it's gone dormant. Natural precipitation is typically enough at this point in the season.

What about sod that was installed later in fall?

If it hasn't fully gone dormant and the ground isn't frozen yet, it may still benefit from some watering support, just far less than it needed right after installation.

How do I know if my lawn has gone dormant?

Dormant grass is brown and not actively growing. If it's already browned off for the season, watering isn't accomplishing much.
