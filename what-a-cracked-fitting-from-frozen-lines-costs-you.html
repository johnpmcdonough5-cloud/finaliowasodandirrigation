What a Cracked Fitting From Frozen Irrigation Lines Costs You

A few fall steps keep your turf strong through the cold months and ready to green up fast in spring.

A cracked fitting sounds like a small problem. What it actually creates once the system runs again in spring usually isn't.

It's Rarely Just One Fitting

If a hard freeze hit water still sitting in the system, it likely affected more than one location, since the same conditions existed throughout the whole network of pipes at once. A cracked fitting found in one zone is often a sign to check the rest of the system rather than assume it's an isolated issue.

The Damage Is Underground

Fittings and pipe joints are buried, which means a crack doesn't just need a part replaced, it needs the ground opened up to reach it. That's a different kind of repair than something visible above ground, and it's part of why freeze damage tends to be more involved than the size of the actual crack suggests.

It Shows Up at the Worst Possible Time

This kind of damage is discovered in spring, right when the system needs to be running for the season and right when a new lawn or landscaping might depend on it working. A repair that could have been entirely avoided with a fall blowout instead becomes a spring priority.

Why Prevention Is the Cheaper Path

A fall blowout removes the water before a freeze has anything to damage. Once a fitting has already cracked, the only path forward is locating and repairing it, which takes more time and effort than winterizing would have taken in the first place.

Common questions

If I find one cracked fitting, could there be more damage elsewhere?

It's possible. The same freeze conditions affected the whole system, so one cracked fitting is a reason to check other zones as well.

Why is this kind of repair more involved than it sounds?

Fittings are buried, so reaching one to repair it means opening up the ground around it, not just swapping a visible part.

Is there a way to know I have freeze damage before spring?

Not always. Most freeze damage isn't discovered until the system is run again in spring, which is why prevention through a fall blowout matters.
