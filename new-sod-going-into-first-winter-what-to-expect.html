New Sod Going Into Its First Winter: What to Expect

A few fall steps keep your turf strong through the cold months and ready to green up fast in spring.

If you installed sod this fall, winter is going to be its first real test. Here's what's normal and what isn't.

Browning Is Normal, Not a Failure

Cool-season grass goes dormant and browns for winter whether it was installed last week or has been established for ten years. If your new sod loses its green color as the weather turns cold, that's the expected dormancy response, not a sign the installation failed.

Why Root Establishment Before Freeze Matters Now

The real question for new sod isn't how it looks going into winter, it's whether the roots had time to anchor into the soil beneath it before the ground froze. Sod that established well before the first hard freeze, which typically hits early to mid October in eastern Iowa, has a working root system in place through dormancy, ready to resume growth in spring.

Keeping Foot Traffic Off Dormant Sod

Dormant grass is more fragile than it looks. Regular foot traffic, and especially anything heavier like equipment or repeated paths across the same section, can damage the crown of the plant even though the grass isn't actively growing. Keeping traffic light over winter protects the investment until spring growth resumes.

What to Watch for Once Spring Arrives

The real test comes when the soil warms up again. A lawn that established properly before winter should green up and resume growth along with the rest of the neighborhood's lawns. Patchy regrowth or areas that don't green up at all are worth a closer look at that point, but that evaluation happens in spring, not now.

Common questions

Is it normal for new sod to turn brown over winter?

Yes. Cool-season grass goes dormant and browns for winter regardless of how recently it was installed.

Should I walk on my new sod during winter?

Keep foot traffic light. Dormant grass is more fragile than it looks, and repeated traffic can damage it even though it isn't actively growing.

How will I know if my new sod actually made it through winter okay?

The real test is spring green-up. Sod that established well before the freeze should resume growing along with the rest of the neighborhood's lawns.
